Room Clima: method

What it is for

Testing a room and its facade at concept stage. The user gives a room type (dwelling, office, classroom), its size, orientation and position (one facade, a corner, a corner under the roof), and the options to compare: window size, glazing, fixed shading, blinds, thermal mass, insulation, ventilation by windows, ceiling fans and, where needed, a chilled ceiling. For each combination Room Clima computes overheating, delivered energy, daylight and whole-life carbon, ranks the designs against fixed targets, and reruns the best ones under future warming and poor operation. The result is a short list of facades that work, with the reasons.

Every constant, with its unit, its status and its source, is defined once in room/src/register.js. Status is one of standard (taken from the text of the standard named), secondary (from a secondary source, to be replaced by the standard text), decision (a modelling choice of this app, dated) or assumption (a typical value without a binding source). The app lists the whole register under "Method and limits". This page explains how the parts fit together.

Ventilation by windows. Air flow each hour by the EN 16798-7 single-sided method, from the effective opening, the sash height and the indoor-outdoor temperature difference. Wind is not credited, because site wind is not known at concept stage; the method underestimates on purpose. The openable sash is half the window area. Day ventilation opens when the room air is above 23 °C, the outdoor air at least 1 K cooler and at least 12 °C. Night ventilation runs after a warm day (operative above 24 °C in the last presence period), when the room is at least 2 K warmer than outside. The flow is reduced so that the room does not fall below the heating setpoint plus 2 K. Workplaces are also checked against ASR A3.6 (depth of single-sided rooms, opening per person, purge opening).

Blinds. In every run, exterior and interior blinds come down when the sun on the window exceeds the DIN 4108-2 threshold (8.4.2 j). Their solar factor F_C is from DIN 4108-2 Table 7. The same hours are used for daylight.

Fans and chilled ceiling. Fans run in occupied hours from 25 °C operative and raise the upper comfort limit by 1.8 K (EN 16798-1 air speed allowance at 0.9 m/s). A chilled ceiling is sized from product data (EN 14240 rating, up to 75 W/m² of floor at 10 K) with water at 16 °C and a dew point margin of 1 K.

Overheating is counted as degree-hours above the band in occupied hours; up to 5 % of occupied hours above the band is accepted without cooling (informative figure of EN 15251). In Germany, the DIN 4108-2 summer check runs with the standard's own conditions (schedules, gains, air change, blinds, reference temperature by summer region A, B, C, and limits of 1,200 or 500 K·h/a). On any weather file other than the test reference year of the region, the result is an estimate, and the app says so. The text of DIN 4108-2 is not part of this repository.

Daylight

Climate-based daylight for the box room, in the browser (room/src/core.js, first part):

  • Daylight coefficients from a radiosity solution of the six room surfaces. Sky and ground in Reinhart patches (MF:2), as Radiance.
  • Sky each hour by the Perez all-weather model with Perez 1990 luminous efficacy, replicated from Radiance gendaymtx.
  • Direct sun by an exact ray test from each sensor through the glazing. Glazing by the Radiance glass model.
  • Blinds as a two-part layer (decision of 20 November 2026): a small share of the light passes straight through, the rest is scattered evenly. Exterior blind 0.10 in all, 0.01 of it straight; interior blind 0.25, 0.03 straight, reflectance 0.6. The angle dependence of slats is not modelled.
  • Useful daylight: share of floor and hours between 300 and 3,000 lx (non-residential) or 100 and 2,000 lx (residential). EN 17037 levels are also reported.
  • Electric lighting: 6 W/m² LED to reach 500 lx (office) or 300 lx (classroom) where daylight falls short.

Checked against Radiance on 6 rooms × 6 climates × 3 blind states in validation/daylight/.

Scores, recommendation and search

  • Scores are on fixed scales, never relative to the run: daylight is the useful daylight share; comfort is 1 with no overheating, 0.5 at the limit; energy is 1 at or below the target and 0.5 at the limit.
  • Recommendation: among the designs that meet every target, the one with the highest score. Ties within 0.01 go to the design with less whole-life carbon. When none meets every target, the closest design, by its largest weighted shortfall. The daylight minimum and DIN 4108-2 are not traded against the rest.
  • Packages: up to five other designs per facade that differ from the recommendation in at least two measures.
  • Search: the option space is searched with pruning; after that, a local search for the design with the least whole-life carbon among those meeting every target. The search was compared with a full enumeration during development; that benchmark is not yet part of this repository.

Scenarios

The recommended design and the packages are rerun:

  • Climate: +2 °C global warming (+3 °C when the run already uses +2 °C). Morphing as in validation/future-climate/.
  • Poor operation: blinds down on only every other day, windows shut at night.

A design is robust when it still meets the comfort limit, or stays the best of the shortlist.

Carbon

Assumptions

  • One room, rectangular, empty. Partitions, floor and ceiling are cut at their mid-plane and exchange no heat with the neighbouring rooms, which are taken to be at the same temperature.
  • Occupants act by fixed rules: windows, blinds, fans and night ventilation follow the thresholds above, every day. The poor-operation scenario tests what happens when they do not.
  • Ventilation by windows is driven by temperature difference only. Wind would add air flow; leaving it out keeps the result on the safe side.
  • Heating and cooling are ideal and all electric, through heat pumps, so delivered energy and carbon follow directly.

Limits

  • The hourly model is tuned to Room Clima's own rooms. In the Standard 140 test box it reaches 32 of 44 ranges and overestimates cooling by 12.7 %; on its own rooms it agrees with EnergyPlus within 0.7 K per hour.
  • Venetian blinds are modelled for daylight as a diffusing layer, without the angle dependence of the slats.
  • The DIN 4108-2 check on a weather file other than the region's test reference year is an estimate.
  • Glare and views are not assessed.
