Method and limits engines, validation, scoring, search

Method and limits

Thermal engine: EN ISO 52016-1 hourly model with wind-driven exterior convection and angle-dependent glazing, validated against ASHRAE Standard 140 and EnergyPlus in 14 climates. Checked against EnergyPlus for the three room types (432 rooms, 3 climates): options ranked alike (rank correlation 0.98), hourly error 0.7 K, rooms about 0.45 K cooler on average and DIN degree-hours about 5% low. Windows sit 10 cm behind the inner face of the wall; the reveal, overhangs and fins shade the glass hour by hour from the same geometry as the daylight model.

Daylight: climate-based, in the page, validated against Radiance (sDA within about 1 point, ASE identical). Scored by one of four measures: useful daylight score S of Reading A (default), useful daylight area, LEED sDA reduced when ASE is above 10%, or the EN 17037 rating as steps; with a minimum below which the score is 0 (EN 17037 minimum by default). Unshaded: movable blinds do not enter the daylight results. Light transmittance by glazing: double 0.80 / 0.70 / 0.55 and triple 0.74 / 0.66 / 0.50 for g 0.60 / 0.45 / 0.30 (typical values, to confirm).

Operation: heating, and ideal cooling in occupied hours to the upper limit of the comfort standard (German annex: the upper edge of its band; EN 16798-1 and ASHRAE 55: their fixed limits for cooled rooms; DIN 4108-2: the region's reference temperature); delivered energy = heating need ÷ COP + cooling need ÷ COP, cooling including moisture removal above 12 g/kg. Overheating is always judged from the run without cooling. The cooled run is a mixed-mode building: windows stay shut while cooling runs, open by day only when the outdoor air is at least 2 K below the cooling limit and at most 12 g/kg, and at night only when it is at most 12 g/kg. The free opening area that day and night ventilation need is estimated for single-sided openings without wind (CIBSE AM10: Q = 0.6 A / 3 √(g h ΔT / T), with ΔT 2 K by day and 3 K at night). Where even the best design is above twice the comfort limit, the climate is taken to need cooling: priorities default to daylight and energy, and overheating without cooling is read as resilience. Internal gains: representative values (office 25, school 30 W/m² in occupied hours, dwelling as DIN 4108-2); the DIN 4108-2 check reruns the designs shown with the standard's own gains. Needs are at the room, per m² floor and year.

Constructions: the room is cut at the mid-plane of the slabs and internal walls, which are adiabatic. Thermal mass levels, heat capacity seen from the room per m² floor (EN ISO 13786, cellular room): light (timber floor on dry screed, gypsum linings, stud partitions) about 100 kJ/m²K; medium (concrete slab with screed and suspended ceiling, sand-lime inner leaf, stud partitions) about 230; heavy (as medium with the slab soffit exposed over 70% of the ceiling, the rest behind acoustic panels and lights) about 390, close to DIN V 18599 "schwer"; very heavy (heavy with 17.5 cm sand-lime partitions) about 700, not tested unless ticked. Floors on an impact sound layer couple only the screed to the room. Mass works mainly together with night ventilation.

Ceiling fans (a design measure): 0.9 m/s, on in occupied hours when the room is at 25 °C or more, 1.5 W/m² floor by default (DC fans; editable in the inputs) of electricity, counted in delivered energy, and of heat. Under EN 16798-1 and ASHRAE 55 they raise the upper comfort limit by 1.8 K, and the cooled room is held 1.8 K warmer; under the German annex only when the EN 16798-1 allowance is chosen (a deviation from the annex); never in the DIN 4108-2 check, which runs without fans.

Cooling need: in Germany (or with the DIN 4108-2 model) the DIN 4108-2 summer protection check decides, run without cooling with the standard's own gains; elsewhere the chosen comfort standard decides: at most 5% of occupied hours above its band by default. Designs are ranked by the degree-hours above the comfort band in either case. In Germany those band degree-hours carry no limit: the 500 K·h/a (1,200 for dwellings) belongs to the DIN 4108-2 check, which counts degree-hours above 25, 26 or 27 °C; the band starts at 24 °C in spring and autumn, so the same room collects several times more degree-hours above it. Compliance with DIN 4108-2 is shown for the designs on the page (with the gains of the standard) and, in the table of all options, from the check at the representative gains, which is stricter. Where cooling is needed: a chilled ceiling without air conditioning is enough when, with dew-point control, its rated capacity (at 10 K between room and mean water, less when the water must run warmer than 16 °C or than the room's dew point + 1 K) covers the dry-weather peak (room at most 12 g/kg; a ceiling rated above the value set is accepted up to 75 W/m² floor), and both the humid hours in which it falls short and the hours with room air above 12 g/kg stay within 5% of occupied hours. Otherwise air conditioning is needed. Ideal loads: a first estimate, not a sizing; they include the fresh air at outdoor temperature.

Two ways of running the room, deliberately different: the run without cooling (overheating, ranking) is a building run without cooling, with windows opened by temperature at any outdoor humidity, as adaptive comfort assumes; the cooled run (energy, loads) is a mixed-mode building that keeps windows shut in humid air and while cooling runs. Adaptive bands are held at their top value above the range of the model, not extended. Ceiling fans are tested only where the comfort standard credits them.

Building-wide measures (glass, wall insulation, mass) are chosen once for all room types tested: the set with the highest mean score over every facade of every room type. Each room type keeps its own window, shading and ventilation choices. Every option includes the wall reveal of the chosen wall depth; "no added shading" means no overhang, fins or frame.

Scoring: each metric scored 0 to 1 (daylight as chosen; comfort: 1 at 0, 0 at twice the comfort limit; energy: 1 at or below the target, 0 at or above the upper value). The overall score is the weighted geometric mean, with the weights set in the priority triangle (16 fixed steps). Corner picks: the option with the smallest largest weighted shortfall, weight 0.6 on one metric and 0.2 on the others. Regret: the best option's score minus the best score found with that measure changed.

Recommendations: the building's facades are combined by taking, for each set of shared measures, each facade's best option with that set, and keeping the set with the highest mean score; with the whole front of each facade found, this gives the building's best option (checked against brute force). A choice is listed "across the building" only when the building has it at every facade of every room type tested; otherwise per room type or per facade. Each statement gives the modelled difference against the same option with only that measure changed (simulated), and how many of seven priority settings keep that choice best at every facade. For a shared measure, the guide says at which facades it matters and at which it makes little difference. "Little difference": every alternative tested changes the result by less than 3 daylight points, 100 K·h/a and 1 kWh/m²a. "Avoid": an alternative with a large difference (five times that) at every facade, like for like. "The trade-off" lists changes that improve one result and worsen another beyond the small differences. When no option scores above 0, the guide says which part is at 0 and gives the ranges found. Only the strategies ticked in the inputs are simulated and recommended.

Blinds: down when the sun on the facade exceeds the threshold of DIN 4108-2 (by use and orientation) and the room air is above 23 °C, so that in winter they let the sun in. The plain DIN rule (sun only) shut out winter gains and made interior blinds look better than exterior ones for energy.

Measures: window ratio 20 to 80%; outdoor shading; window U 1.3 (double) or 0.8 (triple) W/m²K; glass g 0.60, 0.45 or 0.30; walls U 0.24, 0.15 or 0.10 W/m²K (roof 0.20, 0.12, 0.08); mass; blinds; day and night ventilation; roof finish on the top floor. Unrenovated envelopes are not simulated. Search: for each facade of the building (room type × facade direction chosen in the inputs), 60 designs at random, window ratio, outdoor shading and glazing drawn from those meeting the daylight minimum there, then four rounds of 30 variations of options on the Pareto front, then every single change of every design on the front, repeated until the front stops changing (Pareto local search; at most 5,000 designs per facade). Checked against brute force, every combination of measures at 7 facades in 3 climates: the whole Pareto front and the best option for every weighting of the priorities were found in all cases. Boundary conditions follow DIN 4108-2 as quoted in secondary sources. Night ventilation outside presence hours above 23 °C room air (assumption, to confirm). Supply air is outdoor air.

Comfort models: EN 16798-1 and ASHRAE 55 adaptive; German annex (22 °C up to a running mean of 16 °C, then 18 + 0.25 × running mean, 26 °C above 32 °C; ±1, 2, 3 K by category); DIN 4108-2 degree-hours, region set from the warmest monthly mean of the weather file (to verify). Not included: humidity in the comfort metric (reported separately), construction price and carbon.
