Method and limits

Thermal engine: EN ISO 52016-1 hourly model with wind-driven exterior convection and angle-dependent glazing, validated against ASHRAE Standard 140 and EnergyPlus in 14 climates. Checked against EnergyPlus for the three room types (432 rooms, 3 climates, v15 controls): options ranked alike (rank correlation 0.98), hourly error 0.7 K, rooms about 0.45 K cooler on average and DIN degree-hours about 5% low. The comparison is to be repeated with the v16 controls. Windows sit 10 cm behind the inner face of the wall; the reveal, overhangs and fins shade the glass hour by hour from the same geometry as the daylight model.

Internal gains and schedules (interne Wärmeeinträge, Nutzungszeiten): every run uses those of DIN 4108-2, 8.4.2 b and e: dwellings 100 Wh/m² a day over all hours; offices and schools 144 Wh/m² a day over 7 to 18 h, Monday to Friday, no public holidays or school breaks. The year starts on a Monday. Energy figures use the same until the profiles of DIN V 18599-10 are added.

Summer protection check (sommerlicher Wärmeschutz, DIN 4108-2): each design shown is rerun with the conditions of 8.4.2 only: the standard's gains, all convective; base air change 0.5 1/h for dwellings and 4 A_G/V in use hours, 0.24 1/h outside, for other uses; heating to 20 or 21 °C; day ventilation up to 3 1/h when the room air is above 23 °C and above the outdoor air; night ventilation at the design's level, 2 1/h where windows can be opened at night or 5 1/h with air across storeys or a fan system, when the room air is above the heating setpoint and above the outdoor air, and only with sun protection reaching g_tot of 0.4 or less; blinds by irradiance on the window alone, and not at weekends when operated by hand in non-residential use; no fans, no cooling. Degree-hours above 25, 26 or 27 °C by summer region (Übertemperaturgradstunden), limit 1,200 K·h/a for dwellings and 500 K·h/a otherwise (Table 9). The region is chosen from figure 1 of the standard. The proof uses the 2011 test reference year of the region (Testreferenzjahr, 8.4.2 c); on any other weather file, the bundled ones included, the result is an indication. Elsewhere in the page the DIN figure of the design run is an estimate: same gains, the app's own controls.

Design runs (comfort and energy): windows open by day when the room air is above 23 °C, the outdoor air is at least 1 K cooler and at least 12 °C; at night after a warm day (operative temperature above 24 °C in the last presence period) when the room air is above the heating setpoint and at least 2 K warmer than outside. In both cases the air flow is reduced in the hour so that the operative temperature stays at least 2 K above the heating setpoint: windows never open while heating runs. Tested on 864 cases in three climates: no hour with open windows and heating; window ventilation adds at most 0.3 kWh/m²a of heating in medium and heavy rooms and up to 0.8 kWh/m²a in light rooms, where the cooled structure draws heat the next morning. Day ventilation: none or 3 1/h. Night ventilation: none, 2 1/h, or 5 1/h, the last only when ticked, since it needs air across storeys or a fan system. Blinds come down by the DIN 4108-2 irradiance thresholds when the room air is above 23 °C, so winter sun is kept; set "by hand" in the inputs, they stay up at weekends in non-residential use. The free opening area that ventilation needs is estimated for single-sided openings without wind (CIBSE AM10: Q = 0.6 A / 3 √(g h ΔT / T), ΔT 2 K by day and 3 K at night).

Comfort models: German annex (DIN EN 16798-1/NA): comfort temperature 22 °C up to 16 °C hourly mean outdoor temperature, then 18 + 0.25 θe, 26 °C from 32 °C; band ±1, ±2, ±3 K by category. Until v15 the band followed the running mean, which roughly doubled the degree-hours above it. The basis on the hourly temperature is in the standard (caption of figure NA.2); the formula and tolerances are from secondary sources until checked against the text of NA.4.1. EN 16798-1 and ASHRAE 55 adaptive models as before; their bands are held at the top value above the range of the model. Ceiling fans raise the upper limit by 1.8 K in the hours in which they run (until v15: in every hour at 25 °C or more).

Operation for energy: heating, and ideal cooling in occupied hours to the upper limit of the comfort standard (German annex: the upper edge of its band; EN 16798-1 and ASHRAE 55: fixed limits for cooled rooms, 26 °C by default; DIN 4108-2: the region's reference temperature). Delivered energy = heating need ÷ COP + cooling need ÷ COP + fan electricity, cooling including moisture removal above 12 g/kg. The cooled run is a mixed-mode building: windows stay shut while cooling runs, open by day only when the outdoor air is at least 2 K below the cooling limit and at most 12 g/kg, at night only when it is at most 12 g/kg. Overheating is always judged from the run without cooling.

Cooling need: in Germany (or with the DIN 4108-2 model) the summer protection check decides; elsewhere the chosen comfort standard: at most 5% of occupied hours above its band by default. Where cooling is needed, the loads are sized on the 99th percentile of the occupied cooling hours (the load exceeded in 1% of them), so a Monday morning after a hot weekend does not set the size. A chilled ceiling without air conditioning is enough when, with dew-point control, its rated capacity (at 10 K between room and mean water, less when the water must run warmer than 16 °C or than the room's dew point + 1 K) covers that load with the room at most at 12 g/kg (up to 75 W/m² floor), and the humid hours in which it falls short and the hours with room air above 12 g/kg each stay within 5% of occupied hours. Otherwise air conditioning is needed. Ideal loads are a first estimate, not a sizing; they include the fresh air at outdoor temperature.

Daylight: climate-based, in the page, validated against Radiance (sDA within about 1 point, ASE identical). Scored by one of four measures: useful daylight score S (default), useful daylight area, LEED sDA reduced when ASE is above 10%, or the EN 17037 rating as steps, with a minimum below which the score is 0 (EN 17037 minimum by default). Movable blinds do not yet enter the daylight results (planned for the next phase). Light transmittance by glazing: double 0.80 / 0.70 / 0.55 and triple 0.74 / 0.66 / 0.50 for g 0.60 / 0.45 / 0.30 (typical product values, an assumption).

Constructions: the room is cut at the mid-plane of the slabs and internal walls, which are adiabatic. Thermal mass levels, heat capacity seen from the room per m² floor (EN ISO 13786, cellular room): light (timber floor on dry screed, gypsum linings, stud partitions) about 100 kJ/m²K; medium (concrete slab with screed and suspended ceiling, sand-lime inner leaf, stud partitions) about 230; heavy (as medium with the slab soffit exposed over 70% of the ceiling) about 390, close to DIN V 18599 "schwer"; very heavy (heavy with 17.5 cm sand-lime partitions) about 700, not tested unless ticked. Shading factors F_C from DIN 4108-2 Table 7. Mass works mainly together with night ventilation.

Building-wide measures (glass, wall insulation, mass) are chosen once for all room types tested: the set with the highest mean score over every facade of every room type. Each room type keeps its own window, shading and ventilation choices. Every option includes the wall reveal of the chosen wall depth.

Scoring: each metric scored 0 to 1 (daylight as chosen; comfort: 1 at 0, 0 at twice the comfort limit; energy: 1 at or below the target, 0 at or above the upper value). The overall score is the weighted geometric mean, with the weights set in the priority triangle. Corner picks: the option with the smallest largest weighted shortfall, weight 0.6 on one metric and 0.2 on the others. Regret: the best option's score minus the best score found with that measure changed. The relative scales (energy from the run, comfort from the run in Germany) are to be replaced by fixed targets in the next phase.

Recommendations: the building's facades are combined by taking, for each set of shared measures, each facade's best option with that set, and keeping the set with the highest mean score (checked against brute force). Each statement gives the modelled difference against the same option with only that measure changed. "Little difference": every alternative tested changes the result by less than 3 daylight points, 100 K·h/a and 1 kWh/m²a. "Avoid": a large difference (five times that) at every facade. Only the strategies ticked in the inputs are simulated and recommended.

Search: for each facade of the building, 60 designs at random, window ratio, outdoor shading and glazing drawn from those meeting the daylight minimum there, then four rounds of 30 variations of options on the Pareto front, then every single change of every design on the front, repeated until the front stops changing (at most 5,000 designs per facade). Checked against brute force at 7 facades in 3 climates (v3).

Weather files: gaps in air temperature, dew point and radiation are filled by linear interpolation in time and listed under the weather input; a file with more than 2% of hours missing in any of them is refused. Missing long-wave sky radiation is computed from dew point and sky cover (Clark and Allen). The bundled German file is a test reference year whose edition is not recorded; it stands for region C.

Not included yet: humidity in the comfort metric (reported separately), construction cost and carbon, obstruction by neighbouring buildings, future climate. Supply air is outdoor air.

Parameter register

Every value the engine and this page use, with its source. Standard: from the text of the standard named. Secondary: from a secondary source, to be checked against the standard. Decision: a modelling choice of this app, dated. Assumption: a typical value without a binding source.
